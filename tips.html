01 — Skin Care

Skincare basics for oily, tropical skin

Humidity and heat mean more oil, more sweat and more breakout triggers. Keep the routine short and consistent.

Cleanser twice a day — not every time you sweat

Wash in the morning and before bed with a gentle, pH-balanced cleanser. Between workouts, rinse with water only. Scrubbing too often strips the skin and makes it produce even more oil.

Oily still needs moisture

Oil ≠ hydration. After cleansing, use a light, oil-free gel or lotion. Skin that is dehydrated compensates by over-producing oil — a common reason shine comes back by noon.

Patch-test before you commit

Try a new product on a small patch of skin (behind the ear or inner arm) for three nights. If there is redness, stinging or itching, skip it — and never stack three new actives at once.

Dark spots fade slowly — give it 8 to 12 weeks

Post-acne marks (PIH) respond first to daily sunscreen, then to ingredients like niacinamide, vitamin C or a gentle retinoid. If a mark changes shape or bleeds, have it checked.

02 — Personal Hygiene

Daily hygiene that prevents infections

Clean is not only about smell. These four habits stop most skin infections before they start.

Focus on the folds

Shower at least once daily (twice when it's hot or after long commutes). Give extra attention to armpits, under the breasts, the groin area and between the toes, where bacteria and fungus thrive.

Pat dry — moisture is the problem

After bathing, pat skin completely dry, especially between toes and skin folds. Damp skin left covered by clothing is the most common setup for fungal infections like ringworm and athlete's foot.

Handwash at four key moments

Before eating, after using the CR, after handling money or public transport rails, and after touching animals. Twenty seconds of soap and water beats any alcohol bottle alone.

Change out of what's wet or sweaty

Don't sit in a damp uniform or shirt all day. Change socks and underwear daily, air out shoes between wears, and keep spare socks at work or school on especially hot days.

03 — Beauty & Grooming

Beauty routines that respect your skin

Looking good in 34°C weather is possible — the trick is light layers, clean tools and never sleeping in product.

Remove makeup every single night

Sunscreen and makeup both need a proper first cleanse (balm or micellar water), followed by your regular cleanser. Sleeping in product clogs pores and is a fast track to forehead breakouts.

Read labels: non-comedogenic and oil-free

These terms mean the formula is less likely to block pores — useful when sweat and oil are already stacking up. Skip heavy, greasy bases on hot days and choose lightweight, buildable layers.

Clean your tools weekly

Wash brushes, sponges and pillowcases once a week with gentle soap. Never share lip or eye products with friends — that's an easy way to pass bacteria and viral infections around.

Groom with clean, sanitized tools

Trim nails short, shave with the grain using a clean razor, and skip plucking or shaving over active pimples, rashes or sunburned skin. Sanitize tools with alcohol before and after use.

04 — Sun Protection

Sun protection for a country near the equator

The Philippines regularly records UV Index 9–11 around midday. Protection is not vanity — it prevents burns, dark spots, cataracts and early skin aging.

Daily SPF 30–50, PA+++ or higher

Every morning, on face, neck, ears and the back of the hands — rain or shine, indoors or out. UVA rays pass through windows and clouds, and they are what drive dark spots and wrinkles.

Reapply every 2 hours outside

Use about two finger-lengths for face and neck. After swimming, towelling or heavy sweating, reapply immediately — one morning application never lasts a full day outdoors.

Plan around 10 AM to 3 PM

That window carries the strongest UV of the day. Move outdoor chores, errands and exercise to early morning or late afternoon, and take shaded routes when you can.

Cover up: hat, umbrella, sunglasses, sleeves

A wide-brimmed hat, a UV umbrella, sunglasses (yes, eyelids get sun damage) and tightly woven light-colored clothing cut exposure dramatically. Field and commute days deserve UPF-rated fabric.

05 — Heat & Climate Care

Staying well when the weather doesn't cooperate

Summer heat, packed commutes and humid nights all tax the body. Hydration and early action beat recovery every time.

Drink on a schedule, not on thirst

Start with a glass before coffee and keep a bottle within reach. Thirst shows up after you're already behind. Pale yellow urine is a good gauge; dark yellow means drink more.

Know the difference: heat exhaustion vs. heat stroke

Cool, clammy skin with weakness and nausea calls for shade, fluids and rest. Confusion, fainting or hot dry skin with no sweating is a medical emergency — call for help immediately.

Cool down the safe way

Loose, breathable clothing (cotton or moisture-wicking), cool running water on the neck and wrists, and a spot in the shade. Avoid ice directly on skin — it constricts vessels and traps heat.

Keep skin dry to stop heat rash

Shower soon after sweating, wear loose shirts, and avoid heavy creams or oils in hot weather. If prickly heat flares up, cool the area, keep it dry and give it a few days of air.

06 — First Aid & Safety

First response for everyday mishaps

What to do in the first few minutes — and the warning signs that mean it's time to see a professional.

Minor burns: cool water only

Run lukewarm cool water over the burn for 10 minutes, then cover loosely with sterile gauze. Never put toothpaste, ice, butter or egg whites on a burn. Large, deep, or facial burns need a clinic.

Clean wounds before you cover them

Rinse under clean running water and wash around the wound with mild soap — not soap inside it. Cover with a sterile dressing and change it daily. Spreading redness, pus or fever means see a doctor.

Don't scratch mosquito bites

Wash the bite, apply a cold compress and use an anti-itch gel if needed. Scratching breaks the skin and invites infection. Fever with unusual bleeding or severe weakness needs same-day medical care.

Keep a small home kit ready

Sterile gauze, adhesive bandages, antiseptic solution, scissors, a thermometer and oral rehydration salts cover most daily mishaps. Check expiry dates and restock what you use.

⚠ When to seek professional care

See a doctor or dermatologist if a skin condition spreads, doesn't improve after two weeks of consistent care, is painful, bleeds, or comes with fever. For difficulty breathing, confusion or signs of severe dehydration, go to the nearest emergency room.
