Ask the Experts

Reader questions, answered

Real questions sent through our contact page. Want yours featured? Send it over — we answer a few every week.

Q"Do I still need sunscreen if I stay indoors all day?"

If you sit near a window, by a balcony, or go out even briefly, yes. UVA rays pass through glass and are a main driver of dark spots and early aging. On fully indoor days with no window exposure, a lighter routine is reasonable — but on commute days, apply SPF 30–50.

Answered by Dr. Camille Lim, MD · Dermatologist
Q"Is it okay to bathe twice or three times a day?"

Twice a day is fine and often helpful in hot weather — just keep it gentle. Use a mild soap, avoid very hot water, and moisturize if your skin starts feeling tight or flaky. Three or more showers with harsh soap can dry skin and worsen irritation.

Answered by Rafael Santos, RN · Community Health Nurse
Q"My face is oily but feels tight after washing. What's wrong?"

That combination usually means the cleanser is too strong for your skin. Switch to a gentle, sulfate-free or low-foam cleanser, and follow with a light, oil-free moisturizer. Tightness plus shine is dehydration, not "extra dirty" skin.

Answered by Jomar Villanueva · Licensed Esthetician
Q"How much water do I really need during summer?"

Around eight glasses is a reasonable baseline for many adults, but heat, sweat and physical work raise the need significantly. A practical rule: drink before you feel thirsty, keep pale-yellow urine as your target, and add water or an oral rehydration solution after heavy sweating.

Answered by Marife Aquino, RND · Nutritionist-Dietitian
Q"Is toothpaste or baking soda good for pimples?"

No. Toothpaste contains detergents, flavorings and irritants that can cause contact dermatitis and make redness worse. Use a proper spot treatment (benzoyl peroxide or salicylic acid, introduced slowly) and never squeeze lesions — picking is what causes long-lasting marks.

Answered by Dr. Camille Lim, MD · Dermatologist
Q"What's the fastest way to stop underarm odor?"

Odor comes from bacteria breaking down sweat, so the fix is cleansing plus dryness: shower daily, dry the area fully, use an antiperspirant (not just perfume) at night when you're less sweaty, and change out of damp shirts. Persistent odor despite good hygiene is worth a check-up.

Answered by Rafael Santos, RN · Community Health Nurse
